Technical Architecture

Architecture Style: Microservices. This architecture is chosen for its scalability, fault isolation, and independent deployability. Given the complex nature of NLP processing, data integration, and matching algorithms, microservices allow for independent scaling and optimization of each component. This approach also enables different teams to work on different parts of the system concurrently, accelerating development. The ability to update individual services independently supports continuous improvement and reduces the risk associated with large, monolithic deployments. While microservices introduce complexity in terms of deployment and inter-service communication, the benefits of scalability and maintainability outweigh the costs for a platform of this nature.

Stakeholder Views

💰

CFO

This initiative presents a compelling investment opportunity centered on a high-margin, recurring revenue model. Our primary revenue stream is an Enterprise B2B SaaS subscription, projected to contribute 85% of total revenue, targeting large hospital systems with significant patient volume. This model, priced at $54,000 annually per institution, ensures predictable cash flow and high lifetime value. The secondary Professional tier and a long-term Pharma Data Insights stream provide revenue diversification. The cost structure is weighted towards initial high-skilled personnel and critical regulatory compliance, particularly for US market entry under HIPAA. While the upfront investment is considerable, the financial model projects a strong ARPU of $3,875 and a favorable LTV/CAC ratio once market fit is established. The go-to-market strategy mitigates risk by focusing on a specific beachhead market in Australia to validate our core financial assumptions before scaling capital-intensive US operations.

Anticipated Objections

Q: The LTV and CAC figures are based on hypotheses and assumptions. How can we trust the ROI projections?

A: You are correct; these are our top validation priorities. The GTM plan is designed to address this directly by focusing on a contained beachhead market where we will run targeted sales and marketing campaigns to establish a validated CAC. Early customer contracts will allow us to calculate a data-driven LTV within the first 12 months, before we commit to more significant expansion capital.

Q: Revenue is heavily concentrated (85%) in the Enterprise tier, which typically has long and complex sales cycles. This introduces significant risk to our revenue forecast.

A: This concentration is a known risk. Our strategy addresses this in two ways: first, by deploying a specialized Direct Sales team focused exclusively on these accounts to manage the complex sales process. Second, the Professional SaaS tier serves as a lower-friction entry point to capture revenue from smaller practices and de-risk our dependence on the enterprise sales cycle.

Q: The estimated compliance cost of $100K-$250K for HIPAA alone seems high for an early-stage venture. Is this cost necessary and fully budgeted?

A: This cost is both necessary and accounted for in our financial model. HIPAA compliance is a non-negotiable requirement for US market entry, and failure to comply carries severe financial and reputational penalties. This budget line item is a critical investment to de-risk the business legally and operationally for our largest target market.

🔒

CISO

This plan correctly identifies data security and regulatory compliance as paramount to our success, particularly given our handling of Protected Health Information (PHI). The initial assessment has flagged HIPAA and Australian TGA regulations as applicable, with a corresponding high-impact risk assigned to data privacy in our risk matrix. This demonstrates a foundational understanding of the security landscape we are entering. The proposal allocates a specific budget and timeline for achieving compliance, including the implementation of necessary administrative, physical, and technical safeguards. The choice of a microservices architecture, while increasing the attack surface, is acknowledged, and the mitigation strategy will require a robust security-by-design approach, including zero-trust principles and comprehensive monitoring. The 'Pharma Data Insights' revenue stream is noted as a high-risk area requiring stringent anonymization and data governance protocols before activation.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Identifying regulations is the easy part. The plan lacks a detailed implementation roadmap for achieving compliance.

A: This is a valid point. The current plan serves as a strategic framework. The immediate next step, funded by the seed round, is to engage a specialized healthcare compliance consultancy to develop a detailed, actionable roadmap. This will include policy drafting, technical control implementation, and employee training, as outlined in the mitigation strategy.

Q: A microservices architecture creates numerous points of failure and potential intrusion. How will we ensure secure inter-service communication and centralized monitoring?

A: The architecture team has acknowledged this trade-off. Our security strategy for this architecture will involve implementing a service mesh for mTLS, centralized logging and threat detection through an AWS SIEM solution, and strict IAM policies for each service. This is a core requirement for the platform engineering team from day one.

Q: The plan to sell anonymized data to pharma companies is a significant privacy risk and could erode user trust.

A: We share this concern. This revenue stream will not be activated until we have a mature and independently audited data governance framework. This includes a rigorous, multi-stage de-identification process and a clear policy that is transparent to our users. The primary SaaS business must be successful and trusted before we even consider this secondary stream.

🏛️

ARB

The proposed technical strategy is centered on a microservices architecture, a logical choice for a system requiring high scalability, fault isolation, and independent component evolution, particularly for the NLP and data integration services. The technology stack is modern and well-supported, leveraging React for the frontend and Python for backend/ML services, running on AWS for its robust managed services ecosystem. This stack aligns with available talent pools and industry best practices. The primary technical challenge identified is the integration with diverse and unstructured Electronic Health Records (EHRs), which is appropriately prioritized as the first and most critical epic. The plan acknowledges the complexity, starting with common formats like PDF and HL7, with the intent to build more sophisticated integrations over time. The architecture's success will be contingent on building a strong DevOps and MLOps culture to manage the inherent complexity of a distributed system.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Microservices introduce significant operational overhead. Is the team provisioned with the necessary DevOps expertise to manage this complexity?

A: This is a critical dependency. The personnel plan explicitly budgets for experienced NLP/ML and DevOps engineers from the start. We will leverage AWS managed services like EKS and Lambda to offload some operational burden, but a core competency in CI/CD, infrastructure-as-code, and observability is a non-negotiable part of our hiring plan.

Q: The plan is vague on EHR integration, which is the hardest part of this problem. A promise to support 'common formats' is not a strategy.

A: We agree this is the crux of the technical challenge. The strategy is to de-risk this by tackling it iteratively. Our GTM focus on major academic hospitals allows us to initially target a smaller set of dominant EHR systems (e.g., Epic, Cerner) and their specific integration points. The initial epic focuses on building a flexible data extraction pipeline that can be adapted and expanded as we onboard new hospital systems.

Q: The NLP component is presented as a solved problem, but its accuracy will be critical for clinical trust. How will we handle model validation and accuracy?

A: The NLP model is not a fire-and-forget component. The product roadmap includes features for 'explainable AI', allowing clinicians to see exactly which data from the EHR led to a potential match. We will also incorporate a 'human-in-the-loop' feedback mechanism for clinicians to correct or validate extractions, which will be used to continuously retrain and improve our models.

📦

VP Product

This initiative is squarely focused on a high-value, validated market problem. Our market intelligence confirms that oncologists are burdened by administrative tasks, and patients are missing opportunities for potentially life-saving treatments. We are addressing a critical pain point for our primary persona, Dr. Eleanor Vance, whose primary frustration is the time-consuming manual screening of patients for trial eligibility. Our core value proposition is to drastically reduce this time, allowing her to focus on patient care and research. The product strategy is to start with the most critical 'job to be done': quickly identifying relevant trials for a new patient. The initial epic, 'Patient EHR Analysis,' is the cornerstone of our differentiation, using NLP to automate the extraction of complex clinical data. This provides a clear competitive advantage over solutions that rely on structured data or manual input. Our GTM strategy of starting with a focused beachhead allows us to work closely with initial users to refine the product and ensure strong product-market fit before scaling.

Anticipated Objections

Q: The evidence for these 'critical' pain points is largely based on hypotheses (E4) and industry data (E3), not direct user research. How do we know we're solving the right problem?

A: This is the most significant gap in our current evidence. The immediate priority is to launch a product discovery phase, running in parallel with technical setup. This involves conducting deep, qualitative interviews with 20-30 oncologists and trial coordinators in our Australian beachhead market to rigorously validate these hypothesized pains before committing significant engineering resources.

Q: Clinicians are often resistant to new technology, especially 'AI' they don't trust. What is our strategy to overcome adoption hurdles?

A: Our strategy is to position the product as an intelligent assistant, not an automated decision-maker. The user interface will be designed for transparency, showing the clinician exactly which pieces of evidence from the EHR support a potential trial match. Building trust is paramount, and early usability testing will focus on ensuring the tool feels empowering, not prescriptive.

Q: The market is described as 'fragmented'. This could mean it's crowded with point solutions. How do we avoid being just another tool in a complex workflow?

A: Our differentiation lies in the depth of our integration. By automatically processing unstructured EHR data, we eliminate the manual data entry required by many competing tools. This saves significant time and surfaces candidates who might be missed otherwise. We are not just a search portal; we are an integrated screening automation platform, which addresses a more fundamental workflow problem.
