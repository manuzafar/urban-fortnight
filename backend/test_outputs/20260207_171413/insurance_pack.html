Pain Signals

Extended claim processing times cause significant stress and inconvenience for policyholders. Customers often wait weeks or months for claims to be resolved, delaying repairs or replacements.

Lack of transparency throughout the claims process leaves customers feeling uninformed and powerless. They struggle to understand the status of their claim, the reasons for delays, and the basis for settlement offers.

Customers suspect that insurers are deliberately trying to minimize payouts or deny legitimate claims. This distrust is fueled by complex policy wording, perceived lack of empathy, and aggressive fraud detection measures.

The increasing frequency and severity of extreme weather events are causing inbound claim surges, overwhelming insurers' capacity and further delaying claim processing. This is compounded by shortages in skilled trades needed for repairs.

Customers find it difficult to provide all the required information and documentation for their claim, especially when dealing with complex or emotionally distressing situations. This can lead to delays and increased friction in the claims process.

Technical Architecture

Architecture Style: Microservices. This architecture allows for independent scaling, deployment, and fault isolation of individual services. It aligns well with the need for rapid iteration, diverse technology choices for different components (e.g., AI models requiring specialized infrastructure), and the ability to handle varying loads on different parts of the system (e.g., claim intake vs. fraud detection). While more complex initially, it provides better long-term maintainability and scalability than a monolith, and is more appropriate for a team of the described size.

The architecture consists of several microservices communicating via APIs. The Claims Intake Service handles mobile app submissions. The Damage Assessment Service uses AI to evaluate damage. The Fraud Detection Service scores claims in real-time. The Claims Processing Service manages the workflow. The Integration Service connects to external repair networks and other insurance systems. An API Gateway acts as the single entry point. Data is stored in a PostgreSQL database and Redis cache is used

Anticipated Objections

A: We acknowledge the complexity. Our strategy is to manage this by heavily leveraging AWS managed services to handle orchestration, database management, and networking. Q: React Native can introduce performance bottlenecks and abstraction layer issues not present in native development.

A: Correct. We evaluated this trade-off and determined that for our application, which is primarily forms-based and workflow-driven, the development velocity gained from a single codebase outweighs the potential for performance issues. We will mitigate this risk by profiling performance on critical user paths and can write native modules for any specific feature that requires it.

Q: How will you manage inter-service communication and prevent cascading failures in a distributed system?

A: We will use a combination of synchronous (RESTful APIs for direct requests) and asynchronous (event-based messaging via Kafka/AWS SNS/SQS) communication patterns. To prevent cascading failures, we will implement standard resilience patterns like circuit breakers, retries, and timeouts within our service mesh.
