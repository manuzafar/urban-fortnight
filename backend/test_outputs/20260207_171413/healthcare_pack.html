Pain Signals

Oncologists spend excessive time searching for suitable clinical trials for their patients, taking away from direct patient care.

Trial coordinators are overwhelmed with screening potential patients, many of whom are ineligible, creating a bottleneck in the enrollment process.

Patients are often unaware of available clinical trial options, leading to missed opportunities for potentially life-saving treatments.

The unstructured nature of EHR data and clinical trial protocols makes accurate matching difficult, even with AI-powered solutions. False positives can waste coordinator's time.

Different hospitals and institutions have varying levels of EHR adoption and data standardization, making it difficult to integrate and analyze patient data across multiple sites.

Technical Architecture

Architecture Style: Microservices. This pattern allows for independent scaling and deployment of services, crucial for handling varying workloads (NLP processing vs. API serving). It improves fault isolation, allowing individual services to fail without bringing down the entire system. The team size (small initially) can focus on specific services, and the increased complexity is justified by the long-term scalability and maintainability benefits, especially considering the need for regulatory compliance and data security.

The system is composed of several microservices communicating via REST APIs and message queues. The EHR Ingestion Service receives patient medical records and pushes them to a message queue. The NLP Service consumes messages from the queue, processes EHRs using NLP, and stores extracted data in a dedicated database. The Clinical Trial Matching Service retrieves patient data from the NLP database and trial eligibility criteria from the ClinicalTrials.gov Integration Service. It performs matching,

Stakeholder Views

💰

CFO

This initiative presents a compelling investment case by targeting a high-severity, validated pain point in the oncology market with a diversified revenue model. Our primary revenue stream is a scalable B2B SaaS subscription for hospitals and clinics, projected to contribute 65% of total revenue. This is supplemented by performance-based fees from pharmaceutical partners and high-margin data licensing, creating a robust and defensible financial structure. The go-to-market strategy focuses on a capital-efficient beachhead approach in major Australian oncology centers, allowing us to validate key financial assumptions like CAC and LTV before scaling internationally. The financial model projects a path to profitability based on tiered subscription pricing that aligns value with customer size, from small clinics to large cancer centers. While market competition exists, our focus on clinician workflow integration provides a distinct competitive advantage. The primary financial risks are centered on the assumptions underpinning our CAC/LTV models and market adoption rates, which our phased GTM strategy is designed to systematically de-risk.

Anticipated Objections

Q: The revenue projections, particularly from data licensing, seem speculative and rely on unproven assumptions.

A: This is a valid concern. Our model conservatively estimates the data licensing stream at only 5% of revenue. The business case is primarily built on the predictable, recurring revenue from the SaaS subscription model, which is our core focus. The data stream represents upside potential once we achieve critical mass.

Q: The blended Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of AUD 8,500 seems high and could strain early-stage cash flow.

A: The CAC reflects a direct, enterprise sales model necessary for healthcare. It accounts for long sales cycles and the need for specialized sales talent. We will closely monitor this metric in our beachhead market and optimize channel mix. The high LTV of enterprise clients justifies this initial investment.

Q: The valuation is based on a hypothesis and may not be achievable, posing a risk to future funding rounds.

A: We acknowledge the valuation is a forward-looking projection based on market comparables. Our immediate focus is on hitting the operational milestones—user acquisition, engagement, and initial revenue in our beachhead market—that will validate this valuation and build a strong case for subsequent investment.

🔒

CISO

This project inherently involves the handling of highly sensitive electronic Protected Health Information (ePHI), placing security and compliance at the forefront. Our strategy is to build a secure-by-design platform, addressing regulatory requirements like HIPAA from day one. The technical architecture, based on microservices, supports this by allowing for stringent security controls and fault isolation for services that process ePHI. Key compliance activities, such as implementing technical safeguards, entering into Business Associate Agreements (BAAs), and establishing breach notification protocols, are explicitly planned with a 6-9 month timeline. Data will be encrypted in transit and at rest, and all access will be logged for auditing, as specified in our initial product requirements. The most significant risk vector is the integration with third-party EHR systems, which we will mitigate through secure import protocols and by limiting initial integrations to major, trusted vendors. Our risk management plan includes proactive measures to prevent, detect, and respond to potential data breaches, ensuring we meet our legal and ethical obligations to protect patient data.

Anticipated Objections

Q: A microservices architecture can increase the attack surface and complexity of security management.

A: While the surface area is larger, this architecture allows us to apply targeted, stringent security controls on the specific services that handle ePHI, rather than having to secure a monolithic application. It also improves fault isolation, preventing a breach in one service from easily compromising the entire system.

Q: The plan for EHR integration is a major security vulnerability; we are relying on the security of third parties.

A: This is our highest-priority security risk. Mitigation involves a multi-layered approach: limiting initial scope to established EHRs like Cerner and Epic, using secure data transfer protocols, encrypting all data, and implementing strict auditing for all import activities as defined in our requirements. We will not proceed without a secure, validated connection method.

Q: The 6-9 month compliance timeline for HIPAA seems aggressive and may be under-resourced.

A: The timeline is ambitious but achievable because we are incorporating compliance requirements into the design phase rather than retrofitting them. We acknowledge this requires dedicated resources, and the budget must include provisions for compliance expertise and potential third-party audits.

🏛️

ARB

The proposed technical solution is centered around a microservices architecture, a decision driven by the need for scalability, resilience, and maintainability. This pattern is particularly well-suited to the problem domain, as it allows us to independently scale the computationally intensive NLP processing services from the standard user-facing APIs. This separation improves fault isolation, which is critical when dealing with sensitive healthcare data and workflows. The technology stack is pragmatic, leveraging Python for its mature NLP and data science ecosystem and React for its robust component-based UI development. This allows us to tap into large talent pools and extensive open-source libraries. The primary technical challenge and risk is the integration with diverse EHR systems. Our approach mitigates this by starting with a limited set of major EHRs (e.g., Cerner, Epic) to develop a reusable and secure data extraction pattern before expanding.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Microservices introduce significant operational overhead and complexity, which may be premature for an early-stage product.

A: We recognize the added complexity. However, the clear separation between the data-intensive NLP pipeline and the user-facing application provides immediate benefits in terms of independent scaling and deployment that outweigh the overhead. This choice anticipates future needs and avoids a costly re-architecture down the line.

Q: The plan to support 'various EHR systems' is vague and understates the immense technical challenge.

A: This is the core technical risk. The initial product epic, EPIC-001, explicitly scopes this down to supporting just two major EHR systems initially. The goal is to build a robust, secure, and repeatable integration pattern for these two before we commit to supporting a wider variety. We will leverage standards like FHIR where possible but assume custom connector development will be necessary.

Q: Python may not be performant enough for all backend services, potentially creating bottlenecks.

A: Python is selected for its strengths in data processing and NLP. The microservices architecture provides the flexibility to write other, more performance-critical services (e.g., a real-time API gateway) in a different language like Go or Java if benchmarks prove it's necessary. We are not locked into a single language for the entire backend.

📦

VP Product

This product directly addresses a severe, well-documented pain point for oncologists: the excessive time spent on manual clinical trial matching. Our research indicates clinicians spend 10-20 hours per week on this, detracting from patient care. Our solution is designed for the clinical workflow, using EHR integration and NLP to automate the pre-screening process. This positions us uniquely against competitors like Trialjectory, which are primarily patient-facing and lack deep clinical workflow integration. Our primary persona, Dr. Eleanor Vance, represents oncologists who are time-poor, data-driven, and motivated by improving patient outcomes. The product's core value proposition is giving her back time and providing confidence that she hasn't missed a potential life-saving trial for her patient. The initial product roadmap, starting with Epic EPIC-001, is focused on delivering this core value proposition by tackling the foundational challenge of data extraction and processing, which is the key enabler for intelligent matching.

Anticipated Objections

Q: How do we compete with established players like Trialjectory who already have brand recognition?

A: Our differentiation is our user focus and business model. Trialjectory is a B2C, patient-empowerment tool. We are a B2B SaaS product designed for the oncologist's workflow. By integrating with the EHR, we solve the data entry and search problem for the clinician, which is a fundamentally different and, we believe, more valuable proposition for the healthcare system.

Q: Will oncologists trust an AI's recommendations? Adoption could be a major hurdle.

A: Trust is paramount. The product is positioned as a 'decision support' tool, not a 'decision-making' tool. It surfaces potential candidates and highlights the specific data points that created the match, but the final clinical judgment remains with the oncologist. Early features will focus on transparency and explainability of the matching results.

Q: The success of the product is entirely dependent on EHR integration, which is a huge technical risk. What if we can't deliver it?

A: This is the central product risk. Without effective EHR integration, the core value proposition of saving the oncologist time is severely diminished. That is why it is the focus of our first epic (EPIC-001). If the technical POC for integration fails, we would need to pivot the entire product strategy, potentially to a more manual data-entry model, which would require a full re-evaluation of the business case.
