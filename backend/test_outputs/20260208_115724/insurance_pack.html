Pain Signals

Extended claims processing times frustrate policyholders and increase operational costs. Customers often experience delays in receiving compensation or getting their property repaired.

Inconsistent claims assessments lead to unfair payouts and increase the risk of litigation. Manual assessments are prone to human error and bias, resulting in variations in compensation for similar claims.

Fraudulent claims cost insurers billions of dollars annually, increasing premiums for all policyholders. Current fraud detection methods are often ineffective in identifying sophisticated fraud schemes.

Customers lack transparency into the claims process, leading to distrust and dissatisfaction. Policyholders often feel uninformed about the status of their claim and the reasons for decisions made by the insurer.

Some customers may prefer direct human interaction over a fully automated claims process, particularly for complex or emotionally charged claims. A purely automated system could alienate these customers and lead to lower NPS.

Stakeholder Views

💰

CFO

This initiative presents a compelling investment case with a clear path to profitability, structured around a scalable SaaS revenue model. The primary revenue stream is a tiered subscription service, projected to contribute 80% of total revenue, supplemented by high-margin, usage-based add-ons like advanced fraud detection (15%) and initial implementation fees (5%). The financial model forecasts strong unit economics, with a target LTV well exceeding the projected CAC, particularly as we scale and optimize our channel mix. The cost structure is weighted towards personnel, specifically R&D and sales, which is typical for a growth-stage SaaS venture. Key financial risks are concentrated in market adoption rates and the accuracy of our LTV and market size assumptions, many of which are currently hypotheses. The go-to-market strategy mitigates this by focusing on a specific beachhead market (NSW mid-sized insurers) to validate the model before committing to national expansion and higher capital burn.

Anticipated Objections

Q: The revenue projections seem overly optimistic given the number of assumptions in the model.

A: This is a valid concern. The model's sensitivity is highest on adoption rate and pricing elasticity. However, it is based on a tiered subscription model which allows for significant upsell and cross-sell revenue. The top validation priorities are to confirm market size and willingness to pay, which will de-risk these projections significantly.

Q: A $15,000 CAC for direct sales seems high. How can we ensure a positive ROI?

A: The $15k CAC is for high-touch enterprise sales targeting large annual contracts, making the LTV:CAC ratio highly favourable. Our blended CAC will be lower as we scale more efficient channels like content marketing ($2k CAC). The initial focus on direct sales is necessary to win foundational customers in our beachhead market.

Q: The cost structure is heavily reliant on expensive technical and sales talent. What's our plan to manage burn rate if revenue targets are missed?

A: We've aligned our hiring plan with revenue milestones. The phased geographic rollout, starting with NSW, allows us to control sales and marketing spend. The microservices architecture also allows for scaling development teams on specific modules in a more cost-effective manner.

🔒

CISO

This proposal outlines a security-conscious approach from inception. The selection of a microservices architecture on AWS provides a strong foundation for implementing granular security controls and fault isolation, directly supporting APRA's CPS 230 operational risk standard. We have explicitly identified key regulations, including APRA's CPS 234 for Information Security and CPS 231 for Outsourcing, and these are foundational to the technical and product requirements. The technology stack choices, such as using managed AWS services, help reduce the operational security burden. The roadmap includes provisions for robust identity and access management, data encryption at rest and in transit, and regular security testing. While the plan is comprehensive at a strategic level, the next phase will require a detailed threat model and a compliance matrix mapping architecture components to specific regulatory controls. The risk assessment has identified potential security-related risks, and mitigation strategies are integrated into the project plan.

Anticipated Objections

Q: A microservices architecture increases the attack surface and complexity of security management.

A: We acknowledge the increased number of network endpoints. However, this architecture allows us to apply the principle of least privilege more effectively, with dedicated security controls per service via an API gateway. This also isolates any potential breach, preventing lateral movement, which is a significant advantage over a monolithic application.

Q: The plan mentions APRA compliance, but lacks specifics on implementation and auditing.

A: This inception pack confirms our awareness and strategic alignment. The next step, post-approval, is to develop a detailed compliance roadmap. This will include creating a compliance matrix, integrating automated security testing into the CI/CD pipeline, and scheduling third-party audits and penetration tests prior to launch.

Q: Relying on a single cloud provider (AWS) introduces concentration and outsourcing risk under CPS 231.

A: This is a key consideration. Our architecture will be designed for high availability across multiple AWS Availability Zones to mitigate outage risks. Our contractual agreements with AWS will be scrutinized to ensure they meet APRA requirements, and our internal business continuity and disaster recovery plans will account for provider-level risks.

🏛️

ARB

The proposed technical architecture is modern, scalable, and well-aligned with the business objectives. The choice of a microservices architecture is justified by the need for independent scaling of compute-intensive AI services, fault isolation, and development team autonomy. This approach directly supports the product's requirement for rapid iteration and the ability to deploy updates to specific components without impacting the entire system. The technology stack, centered on React for the frontend and Python for AI/ML backend services, is a pragmatic choice that leverages mature ecosystems and relevant talent pools. The rationale for each component, including alternatives considered, is sound. The architecture explicitly plans for the key challenge of integrating with legacy insurance core systems via a dedicated integration service, which is critical for market adoption. The use of AWS managed services (e.g., EKS, RDS, S3) will accelerate development and reduce operational overhead.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Microservices introduce significant operational and developmental complexity. Is the team prepared for this?

A: Yes, this is a calculated trade-off. The complexity is managed by leveraging Kubernetes for orchestration (via AWS EKS) and establishing strong DevOps practices from day one. The benefits of independent deployment, technological flexibility, and targeted scalability are essential for our long-term product vision.

Q: The plan for integrating with legacy Policy Administration Systems is high-level. This is often the hardest part.

A: We agree this is a primary technical risk. The architecture mitigates this by isolating integration logic into a dedicated 'Integration Service' with an anti-corruption layer. The GTM strategy of targeting mid-sized insurers, who often use a more limited set of modern core systems, also de-risks this in the initial phase. A PoC for a common PAS is a top priority.

Q: Python may not be performant enough for high-throughput, low-latency services.

A: Python was chosen for its unparalleled ecosystem in AI/ML, which is our core differentiator. For services requiring higher performance, the microservices architecture gives us the flexibility to use a more performant language like Go or Java without re-architecting the entire system. We will performance-test critical services early to identify any potential bottlenecks.
