Stakeholder Views

💰

CFO

This initiative targets a clear, high-pain market need with a recurring revenue model projected to contribute 90% of total income. The primary revenue stream is a B2B SaaS subscription for oncology providers, supplemented by a secondary data and analytics offering. The cost structure is heavily weighted towards personnel and essential HIPAA-compliant infrastructure, representing a significant upfront investment. The financial model projects a burn rate of approximately $140,000 per month in the first year, with a total Year 1 cost of $2.16M against a projected revenue of $180,000. While the business case is compelling, it's crucial to acknowledge the current evidence base. The financial projections, including pricing tiers, customer adoption rates, and time to break-even (projected Month 18), are largely based on E4-level hypotheses. The top validation priorities identified in the evidence index are all financial metrics. This represents a calculated risk: investing in building a defensible platform to capture a valuable market, with the understanding that key revenue assumptions must be rigorously tested and validated in the initial go-to-market phase.

Anticipated Objections

Q: The revenue projections seem aggressive and are based on weak evidence (hypotheses).

A: This is a valid concern. The projections are intentionally modeled to show the potential scale of the opportunity. However, they are explicitly flagged as hypotheses needing validation. Our go-to-market strategy, starting with a focused beachhead market in Australia, is designed to test and refine these assumptions—specifically pricing and sales cycle length—with a controlled initial investment before scaling.

Q: The Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of $5k-$10k for direct sales feels low for enterprise healthcare.

A: The direct sales CAC reflects a blended model. While direct outreach to large hospital systems will be at the higher end of that range, our channel strategy also includes lower-cost acquisition through industry conferences and webinars. These channels serve to warm leads and reduce the effort required from the direct sales team, thereby lowering the blended average CAC.

Q: A $2M+ annual burn rate is substantial before we have significant product-market fit.

A: We agree this is a significant investment. The cost structure is driven by two foundational necessities: attracting a top-tier technical and product team, and establishing a secure, HIPAA-compliant infrastructure from day one. Postponing these investments would incur greater technical debt and compliance risk, ultimately hindering our ability to scale and secure enterprise customers.

🔒

CISO

This project's core function involves processing Protected Health Information (PHI) extracted directly from hospital EHR systems, placing it squarely under HIPAA regulations in the US market. The platform will act as a Business Associate to our healthcare provider clients. The technical and regulatory plans explicitly acknowledge this high-risk environment. The architecture is designed to be HIPAA-compliant from the outset, with budget allocated for compliant hosting, and the regulatory section outlines key requirements like implementing technical safeguards, entering into BAAs, and complying with breach notification rules. The proposed microservices architecture aids in risk mitigation by isolating the high-risk services that handle PHI (e.g., EHR data ingestion, NLP analysis) from other parts of the application. This reduces the potential blast radius of a security incident. While the high-level strategy is sound, detailed implementation plans for security controls, data governance, and incident response are necessary next steps.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Stating the need for HIPAA compliance is simple, but the implementation is complex and costly. What's the concrete plan?

A: You are correct; this is a non-trivial undertaking. The plan treats compliance as a foundational requirement, not an add-on. We have budgeted for HIPAA-compliant hosting and identified the need for BAAs and specific safeguards per the Security Rule. The immediate next step post-approval is to develop a detailed security roadmap, including selection of a compliant cloud partner and drafting our specific security policies.

Q: Direct EHR integration is our single greatest point of failure and attack vector.

A: We agree. This is the most sensitive part of the system. The microservices architecture is specifically designed to isolate this functionality. The 'EHR Data Extraction' service will have its own security controls, limited permissions, and will be the only component that interacts directly with hospital systems. All data will be encrypted in transit and at rest, and we will enter into comprehensive BAAs that clearly define security responsibilities.

Q: A data breach seems inevitable. Where is the incident response plan?

A: The current plan acknowledges our obligations under the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule. A detailed, actionable Incident Response Plan is a top priority deliverable for the CISO/Security Lead, to be developed in the first 90 days. This plan will include protocols for identification, containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident analysis and reporting.

🏛️

ARB

The proposed technical solution is a microservices architecture built on a modern, robust technology stack: Python with FastAPI for the backend and React for the frontend. The choice of a microservices architecture is justified by the need to isolate the complex, regulated NLP and EHR integration components, which allows for independent scaling, deployment, and fault tolerance. This approach directly supports the business's need for a secure and maintainable platform that can expand geographically. The technology stack leverages Python's strengths in data science and NLP, which is core to the product's value proposition, while FastAPI provides high-performance API capabilities. React is a sound choice for building the interactive UIs required for the various user portals. The most significant technical challenge identified is the EHR integration, which is appropriately prioritized as the first and most critical epic (EPIC-001) in the product requirements. This epic represents the largest technical risk and will require significant focus to de-risk early in the development lifecycle.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Microservices introduce significant operational overhead and complexity, especially for a small initial team.

A: This is a valid trade-off. We believe the upfront complexity is justified. Isolating the HIPAA-regulated EHR/NLP service from the core application logic is not just a scalability choice, but a critical architectural decision for security and compliance. This separation allows us to apply stricter controls and monitoring to the sensitive data service while allowing the user-facing applications to evolve more rapidly.

Q: The user story 'integrate with the Cerner EHR system' grossly underestimates the complexity of EHR integration.

A: We completely agree. The user story title is a simplification for the epic. The high story point estimate (8) for this initial story reflects this complexity. This epic (EPIC-001) is our top priority because it carries the most risk. Our plan is to build a dedicated team for this and create a proof-of-concept with a sandbox environment before we write a single line of application code.

Q: Why not use a more established enterprise stack like Java/Spring or .NET, which are common in healthcare IT?

A: While those stacks are viable, our choice of Python was deliberate. The core value of our platform lies in our NLP and data analysis capabilities. Python is the de facto standard for the data science community, giving us access to the best libraries, tools, and talent. FastAPI provides the performance we need for our API layer, making the Python ecosystem a superior choice for our specific use case.

Anticipated Objections

Q: This market seems crowded. How can we win against established players like Trialjectory?

A: Our differentiation is our business model and point of integration. Competitors are primarily B2C, focused on patient empowerment. We are B2B, focused on clinician workflow efficiency. By integrating with the EHR, we solve the problem at its source, saving the care team time directly. We are selling shovels to the gold miners, not maps to the prospectors. This focus on the provider is our unique wedge into the market.

Q: Dr. Ainsworth is a hypothesis. What if oncologists don't actually want this or aren't willing to pay for it?

A: This is the most critical product risk. While the persona is a hypothesis (E4), it's built on a foundation of industry-level evidence (E3) about physician pain points. Our GTM strategy is designed to mitigate this risk by starting with deep customer discovery in a small number of target hospitals in Sydney and Melbourne. Validating Dr. Ainsworth's pains and her organization's willingness to pay is the primary goal of our first six months.

Q: The first epic is a massive backend integration project. Won't it take too long to deliver tangible user value?

A: For our persona, the backend integration *is* the tangible user value. The output of EPIC-001 is a generated pre-screening package that replaces hours of manual chart review. This isn't just a technical prerequisite; it's the 'magic moment' for our user. We will deliver value by showing them the output of this process on test patients long before the full UI is polished, proving the core value proposition early.

Validation Playbook

1

Oncologist & Coordinator Problem Discovery Interviews

Validates: PS-1

Conduct 30-45 minute semi-structured remote interviews. Use a prepared script focused on their current process for identifying and screening patients for trials. Do NOT pitch the product. Focus on understanding their current workflow, tools, time spent per patient, biggest frustrations, and the impact of failing to enroll patients.

Success: At least 7/10 interviewees independently identify 'manual review of eligibility criteria' as a top 3 workflow bottleneck and report spending >5 hours per week on screening-related activities.
Failure: Fewer than 4/10 interviewees consider trial matching a significant pain point, or they indicate existing solutions are 'good enough'.
2

Concierge 'Human-Powered AI' Matching Service

Validates: T-1

1. Sign a BAA to handle de-identified patient data. 2. Receive 2-3 anonymized patient profiles from each participating oncologist. 3. Manually (using a human expert) parse the patient data and search ClinicalTrials.gov. 4. Produce a 'Pre-Screening Package' for each patient, mimicking the final product's output (top 3 trials, confidence scores, explanation of match). 5. Schedule a 30-minute review session to present the results and gather feedback on accuracy, utility, and time saved.

Success: At least 3/5 participating oncologists agree that the generated package is >90% accurate and would save them at least 1-2 hours of work per patient.
Failure: Participants consistently find major errors in the matches or state the report format is not useful for their workflow.
3

Workflow & Usability Prototype Test

Validates: SV-2

Create a high-fidelity, clickable prototype (e.g., in Figma) that simulates the core user workflow. The prototype should show: a patient dashboard, a list of matched trials, confidence scores, and a drill-down view with matching/mismatching criteria highlighted. Conduct moderated usability tests where users are asked to complete specific tasks (e.g., 'For this patient, find the most suitable trial and review the eligibility criteria'). Record task completion rates and collect qualitative feedback.

Success: 90% of users can complete the core tasks without assistance. Users rate the 'clarity of matching explanation' as 4/5 or higher on average.
Failure: More than 30% of users are unable to complete a key task, or users express significant confusion or distrust in the UI.
4

Pricing & Purchase Intent Survey

Validates: BC-2

Design a 10-minute online survey. First, present the value proposition validated in EXP-1 and EXP-2 (e.g., 'A tool that reduces screening time by X hours and increases enrollment by Y%'). Then, use the Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter to ask four pricing questions ('too cheap', 'a bargain', 'getting expensive', 'too expensive'). Also include questions about their role in the purchasing process and typical budget cycles.

Success: The 'optimal price point' from the Van Westendorp analysis falls within 20% of the financial model's assumed price. At least 30% of respondents identify themselves as having budget influence.
Failure: The acceptable price point is more than 50% below the price needed for the business model to be viable.
5

Patient Demand Smoke Test

Validates: P-3

Create a simple landing page with a clear value proposition for patients: 'Find clinical trials you may be eligible for. Get a personalized report to discuss with your doctor.' Include a single call-to-action: 'Join the Waitlist'. The page should be purely for measuring intent; no product exists behind it. Run the ad campaign for 2 weeks and measure conversion rates.

Success: Achieve a visitor-to-waitlist signup conversion rate of > 4%.
Failure: Conversion rate is < 1%, indicating low patient-driven demand or ineffective messaging.
