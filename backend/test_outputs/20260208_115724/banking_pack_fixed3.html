Technical Architecture

Architecture Style: Microservices. This architecture promotes independent development, deployment, and scaling of individual components, which is crucial for a complex platform integrating with multiple external services and handling sensitive financial data. It allows for faster iteration, improved fault isolation, and the ability to scale specific components based on demand. While it introduces complexity in terms of inter-service communication and management, the benefits outweigh the costs for this project's long-term scalability and maintainability. A hybrid approach might be considered later to incorporate serverless functions for specific event-driven tasks.

The system is structured as a set of microservices communicating via APIs. The user interacts with the platform through web and mobile applications. The API Gateway acts as a single entry point, routing requests to the appropriate microservices. Authentication and authorization are handled by a dedicated Auth Service. Stakeholder Views

💰

CFO

This initiative presents a compelling investment opportunity by targeting a critical, underserved need in the SME market: predictive cash flow management. The financial model is built on a diversified, high-margin revenue strategy, led by recurring SME subscriptions (65% estimated contribution) and supplemented by high-leverage bank partnership licensing (30%) and data insights (5%). Our tiered pricing structure is designed to scale with customer value, capturing a range from $79 to $199 per month, which supports a strong Lifetime Value (LTV) projection. While the initial outlay is focused on product development and market entry, the go-to-market strategy targets a specific, high-value beachhead market to ensure capital efficiency. The key financial assumptions, particularly around Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and LTV, are currently based on market comparables and hypotheses. A core focus of the initial phase will be to validate these metrics to de-risk future investment tranches and ensure a clear path to profitability and a strong LTV:CAC ratio.

Anticipated Objections

Q: The Customer Acquisition Cost and Lifetime Value figures are highly speculative and classified as assumptions or hypotheses.

A: This is accurate. The initial phase is designed specifically to validate these metrics. Our GTM focuses on targeted digital marketing and accounting partnerships, with clear KPIs to measure CAC. LTV will be tracked via cohort analysis of churn and expansion revenue. We recommend a phased funding approach tied to hitting validation milestones for these key metrics.

Q: Our revenue is heavily dependent on the SME subscription model (65%). If adoption is slow, the entire model is at risk.

A: The SME channel is our primary entry point, but the Bank Partnership model is a key strategic hedge. It provides a high-leverage distribution channel that significantly lowers CAC and accelerates market penetration. We plan to initiate conversations with potential banking partners in parallel with the direct-to-SME launch to diversify our revenue risk early.

Q: The projected 5% contribution from 'Anonymized Data Insights' seems low for the effort and potential risk involved.

A: We view this as a strategic, long-term opportunity. Initially, its contribution is conservative. However, as our dataset grows, its value for institutional risk modeling becomes substantial. We are treating it as a valuable option that can be scaled significantly in years 3-5 without distracting from the core business in the early stages.

🔒

CISO

This platform will handle highly sensitive financial data, making security and compliance foundational pillars. The architecture plan explicitly calls out the need to comply with key regulations, including APRA's CPS 234 for Information Security and CPS 231 for Outsourcing, which is critical for our bank partnership channel. We are processing financial data from multiple sources, including direct bank feeds and accounting systems, which elevates our risk profile and necessitates a robust data governance framework from day one. The risk assessment identifies 'Inaccurate Cash Flow Predictions' and potential data breaches as high-priority threats. Mitigation strategies involve stringent data validation, robust access controls, and adherence to security best practices throughout the development lifecycle. The 'Anonymized Data Insights' revenue stream will require a particularly rigorous anonymization and aggregation protocol to prevent data leakage and ensure customer privacy is maintained.

Anticipated Objections

Q: Relying on third-party APIs for bank and accounting data creates a significant supply chain risk that is outside our direct control.

A: Agreed. This is a primary risk. Our strategy is to partner with established, reputable aggregators like Plaid who have robust security postures. We will conduct thorough vendor security assessments and build resilient integration points with comprehensive monitoring and error handling to isolate ourselves from upstream failures or breaches.

Q: A microservices architecture can increase the attack surface and introduce complex security challenges in service-to-service communication.

A: While the attack surface is broader, a microservices approach allows for better fault isolation, preventing a breach in one service from cascading. We will implement a 'zero-trust' network model, mandate service-to-service authentication, and enforce security policies through a service mesh. This provides more granular control than a monolithic architecture.

Q: The 'Anonymized Data Insights' product sounds like a privacy incident waiting to happen.

A: This is a valid concern. This product will only proceed with a strict data governance framework in place. This includes using techniques like k-anonymity and differential privacy, undergoing independent privacy impact assessments, and ensuring our terms of service are transparent. The data will be aggregated to a level where re-identification of any single SME is computationally infeasible.

🏛️

ARB

The proposed technical solution is a microservices architecture built on a modern, scalable technology stack (React, Python, AWS). This architectural choice is deliberate, designed to support the platform's core requirements: seamless integration with multiple external data sources, independent scaling of components like data processing and forecasting, and improved fault isolation to ensure high availability. The initial product epics focus on the most complex technical challenge: 'Data Integration & Processing' (EPIC-001), which is the foundation of the entire platform. While this architecture introduces operational complexity compared to a monolith, it provides the long-term flexibility and scalability necessary to achieve our business goals, particularly as we expand our feature set and integrate with more partners. The technology stack leverages well-supported, industry-standard tools, which will facilitate hiring and reduce ramp-up time. The immediate priority is to de-risk the core integration challenge by building and testing the data pipeline connecting to Xero, MYOB, and bank feeds.

Anticipated Objections

Q: A microservices architecture is overly complex for an initial product launch and will slow down development velocity.

A: This is a calculated trade-off. Given that the core of our product is integrating disparate, independent services, a microservices approach aligns naturally with the problem domain. It allows different teams to work on the Xero integration and the bank feed integration in parallel. We can mitigate complexity by using managed AWS services (like Fargate and Lambda) and investing in CI/CD automation from day one.

Q: The reliability of the entire platform is dependent on external APIs from banks and accounting software, which are notoriously unreliable.

A: This is our primary technical risk. The architecture must be designed for resilience. We will build robust error handling, data reconciliation jobs, and queuing mechanisms to handle API downtime and data inconsistencies. Caching strategies will be employed to ensure the user interface remains responsive even if a background sync fails.

Q: The plan lacks detail on how the AI/ML component for forecasting will be built, tested, and deployed.

A: Correct. The initial focus is on EPIC-001, securing and processing the data. The AI forecasting engine is a subsequent epic. The microservices architecture allows us to develop this component independently. Our plan is to start with simpler heuristic models and gather data, then iterate towards more complex ML models once we have a rich, clean dataset. This de-risks the ML component by separating it from the core data plumbing.
